15:## Follow-up, item 2: meteor shower spawn and impact
17:Native shower operator `0x140726580` clamps magnitude M to [0,1]. It sends one main meteor per recipient, size `max(1,20*M)`, position `target - 30000*d`, velocity `20*d`, where d is the normalized randomized incoming direction. It additionally sends `trunc(10*M)` secondary meteors per recipient. Each secondary aims at independent horizontal offsets in [-500*M,500*M], starts a distance `20000 + U*15000*M` back along d, has size `max(1,(0.1+0.3*U)*20*M)`, and velocity `(19+U)*d`. Each U denotes a separate native random draw `(rand()&32767)/32767`. The per-recipient loops send copies of the same shower, not additional meteors in one client's world. For the shipped M=1 call this is one size-20 meteor and ten size-2-to-8 meteors. Secondary size and speed are drawn inside the recipient loop, so copies need not be identical.
19:These velocities are position increments per scene update. `0x1405e8fb0` adds the stored double velocity directly to position and raycasts the segment from position to position+velocity. At a nominal 60 updates/s the main travels 1200 m/s and secondaries 1140-1200 m/s. The nominal conversion is an inference; update counts and increments are the directly established quantities. It is incorrect to label the stored value 20 as 20 m/s. The main preserves the caller's tsunami flag. Secondary call `0x140726b60`, bytes `e8 6b 61 b0 ff`, supplies false via the fifth argument, so only the main inherits true from the addons.
21:Byte anchors in `followup/meteor-shower-spawn.asm`: `0x140726754`, `f3 44 0f 10 25 6b 9f 48 00`, loads 20; `0x14072693b`, `f3 0f 59 05 09 9d 48 00`, multiplies M by 10; `0x140726943`, `f3 48 0f 2c c0`, truncates the count. `followup/meteor-constants.txt` retains the address, bytes and float32 value of each referenced constant. Event 113 dispatch at `0x14085f718`, bytes `e8 f3 df f1 ff`, reaches `0x14077d710`, which appends a meteor to scene+0xbb1a8 and copies position, velocity, size and flag. `followup/meteor-update.asm` and the scene pseudocode retain the integration and land/water impact branches. Impact sends event 114 with position, size and flag, then removes the meteor.
23:Impact event 114 calls `0x14077d7a0` at `0x14085f73b`, bytes `e8 60 e0 f1 ff`. The false-flag branch calls explosion enqueue `0x14077e720` with size as strength. The true-flag branch passes `3*size`, multiply at `0x14077d7ee`, bytes `f3 0f 59 15 9a 2b 43 00`. Explosion creation `0x140593cd0` caps that strength to 10. Thus the size-20 main reaches explosion strength 10, and the false-flag secondary reaches strength 2-8 at M=1. This strength is an explosion parameter, not HP.
25:The shared explosion update `0x140593ec0` advances age by float32 0.0166599992663 and radius R by `R=343*age`. Its record+0x24 becomes `initial_amplitude*(1-clamp(R/(250*strength),0,1))`. Byte anchors `0x14059417c`, `f3 0f 59 35 7c c6 61 00`, and `0x1405941be`, `f3 0f 11 46 24`, establish radius growth and the amplitude store. The 250 coefficient is at `0x140bb07e8`, bytes `00 00 7a 43`. This amplitude reaches zero at 250*strength, 2500 m for strength 10. It is not a proven block-damage cutoff. In particular body impulse helper `0x140594620` independently applies `min(500000*strength/R,30000*body_mass)` in a shell `(max(0,R-5.714379787))² < distance² < R²`; that expression does not read record+0x24. Tree processing has its own R<20*strength gate. The record also has a 270-count lifetime. Do not extend one of these gates to every consumer.
27:Still open: final block/voxel damage, occlusion and HP falloff; the maximum radius of every explosion consumer; and the ocean-event reach from the true-flag main. Ruled out equating meteor size, particle bounds, the 250*strength amplitude boundary or tree processing range with a universal radius beyond which nothing happens. `followup/explosion-*.asm`, `followup/explosion-constants.txt` and the named function pseudocode retain the distinct paths. `0x14073f890` contains a local-character explosion observer and sound/notification handling, not a demonstrated final voxel-damage equation.

36:Scene update calls collection update `0x140561660`, which invokes `0x14055eff0` for each 0x110-byte volcano. During the active eruption branch the latter calls `0x1405604c0`. This routine has a native spatial query with radius `10 + (eruption_radius-10)*volcano_float[0x68]`. Loads at `0x140560561`, bytes `f3 0f 10 51 44`, and `0x140560572`, bytes `f3 0f 59 51 68`, feed query call `0x1405605a9`, bytes `e8 72 45 c4 ff`. The queried objects undergo further collision-type, random acceptance and update-phase tests. Therefore being in this query radius does not imply taking damage on every update.
38:There is a vehicle-damage-enabled branch, tested against settings+0x4a, that chooses a component/block hit and sends an amount in [500,1000] with scalar `min(amount/2000,4)` via `0x14022c520`. The phase test is `(update_counter + body_index)&15 == 0`. This establishes a direct damage-message path beyond ordinary collisions. It does not yet establish final HP, per-block attenuation or a total damage-per-second equation. `followup/volcano-effects.asm` and `0x1405604c0.c` retain it.

45:## Follow-up, item 4: forest ignition and spread
47:The shipped forest-fire addon only receives objectives. Native tree ignition is `0x140559870`. It writes an intensity byte into a two-byte per-tree state array and appends the tree to its chunk's burning list; a formerly empty chunk is also appended to manager+0xe0. `followup/tree-ignite.asm` retains the stores. Its decoded direct callers are retained in `tools/followup-ignite-callrefs.json`: explosion/tree processing, a direct position/radius ignition helper, spread selection, and the tree-state synchronization path. These establish native ignition, independent of the objective-marker Lua addon.
49:Direct position/radius ignition `0x14055b090` selects a tree inside the supplied squared 3D radius, requiring state flag bit 0 clear and intensity zero, then calls the ignition helper. Scene call `0x1405eae61`, bytes `e8 2a 02 f7 ff`, applies it to an unspent type-3 entry in scene+0xbb288. This is a concrete native ignition producer. The type-3 entry's origin remains unclassified here, so this trace alone is not called proof of a lightning scheduler. Explosion/tree processing `0x14055a970` can also ignite eligible trees; it contains a random draw and random initial intensity 100-199 before call `0x14055addc`, bytes `e8 8f ea ff ff`. The complete explosion ignition probability is still open.
51:The native tree update is `0x14055bf50`, called from manager update `0x140557fa0` at `0x140558581`, bytes `e8 ca 39 00 00`. It samples weather rain and derives integer threshold `trunc(100*rain+100)`. A burning tree's intensity byte increments to 255 while bit 0 is clear; reaching 255 sets bit 0, after which intensity decreases. When intensity is nonzero, exceeds the rain threshold, bit 2 is clear and the tree has not been removed, `rand()%25==0` attempts spread through `0x14055cb70`. This modulo test is not an exactly uniform 1/25 probability for every possible generator range, and is not a per-second probability. The tree loop also samples water and has extinction/removal branches. The update cadence and every eligibility branch remain qualified.
53:Spread selection offsets the source point by `5*manager_vector[+0x70,+0x78,+0x80]`, then searches eligible zero-intensity trees with horizontal squared distance strictly below 100. It chooses one and calls `0x140559870` with initial intensity 1. The vertical chunk test is separate. Thus 10 m is a search radius about an offset point, not a universal fire safe radius. Failed spread sets state flag bit 2, suppressing this tree's further attempts in this branch. `0x14055ce00`, bytes `f3 0f 10 15 88 39 65 00`, loads 100 for squared-distance selection; `0x14055cf5d`, bytes `e8 0e c9 ff ff`, calls ignition. `followup/forest-fire-update.asm`, `forest-spread-target.asm` and the named pseudocode retain the tests. Manager update writes the offset vector using sampled horizontal weather wind; its exact magnitude conversion has not been reduced here.
55:Still open: complete native initial-fire scheduling, explosion/lightning ignition eligibility, exact tree-update frequency, vehicle/character heat and final damage, and total spread reach across multiple generations. The recovered tree intensity is a state byte, not HP or temperature. The objective callback description explicitly says the marker is raised after five burning trees are detected; it is not the ignition rule. `tools/followup-fire-strings.json` preserves its executable string and references. Ruled out the addon marker script as an ignition scheduler, interpreting the 10 m local target search as a whole-fire safe distance, and using the intensity byte as vehicle damage.

67:## Follow-up, item 6: environmental velocity to body force
69:The wind chain already documented above reaches an actual force submission. Surface worker `0x140497970` calls traversal `0x14048ad30` on vehicle+0x738. The air callback `0x14048e2d0` computes point velocity `v + omega cross r - sampled_wind`, runs the normal/viscous/lift terms, and writes a 0x30-byte record containing three double lever-arm coordinates and three double force coordinates. Its list is surface-context+0xab8. These are force contributions, not wind-vector commands. The water callback uses the same record list with its own relative-current subtraction and cap.
71:Postprocessing `0x14048a8f0` iterates that list, sums force into context+0x928..+0x930, and sums `r cross F` into +0x934..+0x93c. The X accumulation is `0x14048aac3`, bytes `f3 0f 11 87 28 09 00 00`; X torque accumulation is `0x14048ab7a`, bytes `f3 0f 11 87 34 09 00 00`. `followup/surface-force-submit.asm` retains the complete loop. Caller `0x14054883d`, bytes `e8 ae 20 f4 ff`, reaches this postprocessor. It submits the summed vector using vtable `0x140b62280`, explicitly RTTI `event_set_impulse_threshold@mm_physics_scene`, and torque using `0x140b9a6f8`, `event_set_torque@mm_physics_scene`. Their pointers, names and apply slots are retained in `followup/surface-force-command-vtables.json`.
73:The first apply slot is `0x1401a17b0`. It calls `0x14019a370` at `0x1401a1866`, bytes `e8 05 8b ff ff`, with the force and a zero lever arm because torque was summed separately. That routine multiplies by Bullet linear factors and adds to total force at body+0x460..+0x470. `0x14019a3a3`, bytes `f2 0f 58 81 60 04 00 00`, is the X addition. The torque apply slot `0x1401a1960` adds the separately accumulated torque to body+0x480..+0x490 after linear/angular factors. Both reject a missing body or body flag 0x800. The event names do not imply an instantaneous velocity impulse. See `followup/surface-force-commands.asm` and `body-impulse-accumulation.asm`.
75:The downstream timestep proof from control-surfaces.md is retained locally as `followup/solver-force-integration.asm` and `solver-time-call.asm`. Solver setup `0x140128560` reads total force, multiplies by inverse mass at `0x140128734`, bytes `f2 41 0f 59 80 60 04 00 00`, and by solver time at `0x14012874b`, bytes `f2 41 0f 59 c0`. It stores an external velocity increment at `0x140128759`. Total force is therefore not velocity, and body+0x3e0..+0x3f0 are linear factors, not inverse mass.
79:Still open: the complete current-grid/cache forwarding and all surface geometry weights, complete shielding eligibility, non-surface wind/current consumers and the SI mass conversion. Ruled out stopping this chain at an unused velocity cache, applying the wing/control-surface k*S cap to the entire hull callback sum, and multiplying the computed force by 60. The main hull force and torque submission is now traced to Bullet and its timestep consumer.
