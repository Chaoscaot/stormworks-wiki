122:## Force application and units
124:Force helper `0x1401a7c70` divides the vector by update argument 7, `S`, limits its length to 100 and multiplies back. For positive finite S, `F_applied=min(F_raw,100*S)` in the unidirectional case above. This is an additional cap, not another jet-size multiplier. S is a grouped vehicle application scale, not proven equal to body mass for all assemblies. The global force-application gate must also be enabled. The rotating exhaust's electrical pivot can change force direction and lever arm without changing the pressure coefficient.
126:The helper queues RTTI `event_set_impulse_threshold@mm_physics_scene`, vtable `0x140b62280`. Handler `0x1401a17b0` calls `0x14019a370`, which adds the vector to Bullet's total-force accumulator. Despite the event name, this is force, not a per-update impulse. Do not multiply the force by 60. The corresponding impulse for one nominal update is force/60. The force handler and accumulator are retained. The existing [control-surface investigation](control-surfaces.md) independently documents the application scale and force accumulator.

143:## Worked examples and graph model
151:An exhaust at incoming pressure 16000, sea level and zero spoiler computes 480000 internal force. With S=1000, the helper limits application to 100000. Half spoiler gives 240000 before the cap. An enabled afterburner with 0.001 stored jet-fuel amount gives multiplier 1.5 and force 720000 before the cap. With 0.4 stored amount, the observed multiplier is 201 and raw force is 96480000. The latter illustrates why assuming a universal 1.5 multiplier would misrepresent the recovered branch; it does not establish a steady externally supplied fuel flow or measured thrust.
